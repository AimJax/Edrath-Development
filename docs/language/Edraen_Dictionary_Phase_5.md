## Purpose and canon boundary

This file records the 65 roots approved by the creator in the fifth Edraen vocabulary batch. All entries below are canon and are included in the master dictionary and Canon Registry.

The batch expands everyday actions, household objects, physical descriptions, community roles, natural materials, foods, and living things. Definitions are lexical only; they do not establish lore, cultural practices, or facts about Edrath.

## Review notes

- The 65 spellings were checked against the previous 270-root approved inventory and established whole terms and proper names recorded in project canon. No exact-spelling collisions were found.
- Exact spelling uniqueness is mandatory. Similarity in sound is a separate design question; the creator approved this batch with the observations below retained for future clarity checks.
- **Sound-similarity audit:** **GELN / GELM / GELV** cluster around existing **GEL** (rough / clay / ladder versus head); **DULV / DULTH / DULN** are close in form (bitter / sour / cheese); **ZELN / ZELR / ZELTH** are close (guard / map / early); **SILN** (loud) is close to **SILR** (read); **MELR** (lush) is close to **MEL** (flower), **MELTH** (ash), **MELN** (ink), and **MELV** (artisan). These are not exact duplicates; the batch was approved with these similarities noted.
- **Semantic distinction:** **VURK** (heal) and **ZURV** (cure) are related but not identical: “heal” concerns recovery or the process of becoming well, while “cure” concerns bringing about recovery from a condition.
- Pronunciation guides are practical reader aids, not a complete phonological specification. The full consonant inventory, permitted syllable shapes, and consonant-cluster rules remain open language-design topics.

## Approval record

The creator approved the complete 65-root batch. At that stage, the roots were included in the 335-root master inventory and Canon Registry; the cumulative approved inventory has since reached 400 roots. Sound-similarity observations are retained as design notes, not duplicate-spelling findings.
