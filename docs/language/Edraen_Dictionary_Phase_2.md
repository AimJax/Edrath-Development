## Semantic boundaries and usage notes

- **NAR / SOV / ELK / URD** name the four cardinal directions. They do not, by themselves, establish map orientation or cultural symbolism.
- **VAD / MOR** mean up and down as directions; **NETH / THUL** mean inside and outside; **RAV / KIR** mean front and back; **SEN / VAN** mean left and right.
- **DUR** remains soil as material; **KEL** remains land or territory. **KORV** means wood as a material, while **TOL** means a tree.
- **VETH** means ice; **KHAL** means snow. **ZUR** means rain; **SUL** means fog or mist.
- **KELT** means a plant’s root; it is not the general word for a plant. **ZAL** means seed; **MEL** flower; **ZAV** grass.
- **MAV** remains the broad word for animal. **ZIVR**, **GUV**, and **KESH** name fish, insects, and reptiles respectively.
- **KATH** means face; **GEL** head. **THUM** means mouth; **KALV** tongue; **NURK** tooth.
- **LETH** means love or deep affection; **YAR** means joy or happiness. **GRATH**, **VURN**, and **METHR** mean fear, anger, and sorrow/grief.
- **RETH**, **ZUL**, **GUL**, **VELR**, and **SKETH** are the five color roots introduced in this batch: red, blue, yellow, white, and gray. No broader color taxonomy is established yet.
