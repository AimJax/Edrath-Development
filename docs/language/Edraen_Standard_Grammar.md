# EDRAEN STANDARD GRAMMAR & USAGE

**Standard:** Edraen Standard v3.0  
**Status:** Approved language standard  
**Purpose:** A practical grammar for everyday conversation, clear prose, dialogue, and connected writing  
**Lexical inventory:** 628 approved roots; grammar words are listed separately

This guide is the primary reference for forming Edraen sentences. The language is designed to be regular: words do not change form for person or tense, grammar words are written separately, and exceptions must be documented rather than improvised.

## 1. The three building blocks

Edraen text uses three kinds of units:

1. **Lexical roots** carry meanings such as person, water, run, remember, good, and city. The cumulative list is in the [Learner's Dictionary](Edraen_Dictionary.md).
2. **Compounds** join roots according to the two approved compound patterns. A compound should have a clear relationship between its parts; do not assign a meaning to a trial compound without documenting it.
3. **Grammar words** are separate short words that show relationships such as I, the, not, past, because, and where. They are not roots and do not count toward the 628-root inventory.

The spelling of a root never changes to show tense, number, person, or grammatical role. Grammar words supply those functions.

## 2. Pronunciation and spelling

### Vowels

- **A** is approximately the vowel in English *father*.
- **E** is approximately the vowel in *bed*.
- **I** is approximately the vowel in *machine*.
- **O** is approximately the vowel in *more*.
- **U** is approximately the vowel in *rule*.
- **AE** is approximately the vowel in *day*.
- **AU** is approximately the vowel in *now*.

The dictionary's recorded pronunciation guide takes priority where a lexical word has an established special pronunciation.

### Consonants and stress

- **TH** is pronounced as in *thin*.
- **KH** is pronounced like the final consonant in Scottish *loch*.
- **NH** in the established root **NHUR** is pronounced as **N**.
- **Y** is pronounced as the consonant in *yes*.
- **G** is hard, as in *go*.
- Stress normally falls on the penultimate (second-to-last) syllable.
- Proper names and roots with recorded pronunciation guides retain those guides.

New roots should be easy to pronounce and spelled consistently. The current inventory does not use C, J, Q, W, or X in native roots. Future additions should not introduce them casually; a deliberate sound-system revision would be needed first.

### Writing conventions

- Dictionary headwords are displayed in uppercase for easy scanning.
- Ordinary sentences may be written in normal sentence case; capitalizing every word is not required.
- Capitalize sentence beginnings and proper names.
- Separate grammar words with spaces.
- Write an established compound as one word.
- Use a period for a statement, a question mark for a question, and an exclamation mark for a forceful command or exclamation. Commas divide longer clauses in the same way they do in ordinary prose.

## 3. Pronouns

Pronouns do not change for subject, object, or possession. Context and word order identify their role.

| Edraen | Meaning | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **AN** | I; me | First-person singular |
| **ET** | you | Second-person; singular and plural are not distinguished |
| **EN** | we; us | First-person plural; inclusive/exclusive meaning is determined by context |
| **IR** | he; she; it; him; her | Third-person singular and gender-neutral |
| **UR** | they; them | Third-person plural |

Examples:

- **AN ZEK IR.** — I see him, her, or it.
- **IR ZEK AN.** — He, she, or it sees me.
- **EN VA RAVN ET.** — We will meet you.
- **UR ZARK THREN.** — They protect people.

### Possession

Place the possessor before the noun. Do not add a possessive ending.

- **AN VAEL** — my heart
- **ET BRUK** — your door
- **EN DOM** — our home
- **UR DASK** — their law
- **AN ZULR** — my book
- **ET ZARV** — your friend

A possessive phrase fills the determiner position, so it normally does not take **LI** or **NA** in front of it. To express a separate “of” relationship, use **OF** after the noun phrase: **LI DOM OF AN ZARV** — the home of my friend.

### Reflexive expressions

Place **SELF** after the relevant pronoun: **AN SELF** — myself; **ET SELF** — yourself; **IR SELF** — himself/herself/itself; **EN SELF** — ourselves; **UR SELF** — themselves.

Example: **AN ZEK AN SELF.** — I see myself.

## 4. Nouns, articles, number, and demonstratives

### Articles

| Edraen | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **LI** | the; a specific or identifiable thing |
| **NA** | a; an; one non-specific countable thing |

Examples:

- **LI DOM** — the home
- **NA DOM** — a home
- **LI SETH DAN** — the good person
- **NA THRAN KRAK** — a large weapon

Articles are not required for every noun. General concepts and mass nouns can appear without one: **AN DUV VEM** — I eat food.

### Plural marking

Use **MA** before the descriptive part of a noun phrase when plurality is not already clear from a number or plural demonstrative.

- **MA DAN** — people
- **LI MA DAN** — the people
- **MA SETH DAN** — good people
- **LI MA SETH DAN** — the good people

A number or plural demonstrative makes **MA** unnecessary:

- **BI DAN** — two people
- **DE DAN** — these people
- **DO DOM** — those homes

Do not add plural endings to roots.

### Demonstratives and universal/other modifiers

**AL** means “all/every” and **OTH** means “other/another.” The plural marker precedes descriptive material: **AL MA OTH SURN** — all other souls. **OTH MA DAN** means other people.

| Edraen | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **DI** | this |
| **DU** | that |
| **DE** | these |
| **DO** | those |

The demonstrative occupies the determiner position: **DI DOM** — this home; **DO MA DAN** — those people. In ordinary use, do not combine a demonstrative with **LI** or **NA**. When the noun is obvious from context, a demonstrative may stand alone as a pronoun: **DI** — this; **DU** — that; **DE** — these; **DO** — those. For example, **KA ES DI?** — “What is this?” and **KI ES DU?** — “Who is that?”

### Numbers

| Edraen | Number | Edraen | Number |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| **ZI** | 0 | **PEN** | 5 |
| **UN** | 1 | **SEK** | 6 |
| **BI** | 2 | **SET** | 7 |
| **TRI** | 3 | **OK** | 8 |
| **KAR** | 4 | **NEN** | 9 |
| **DEK** | 10 | **HUN** | 100 |
| **MIL** | 1,000 | | |

For numbers from 11 to 19, say **DEK** followed by the unit: **DEK UN** = 11; **DEK NEN** = 19. For multiples of ten, say the multiplier before **DEK**: **BI DEK** = 20; **TRI DEK** = 30. Add a unit after the tens when needed: **BI DEK TRI** = 23. For hundreds and thousands, put the multiplier before **HUN** or **MIL**: **BI HUN** = 200; **TRI MIL** = 3,000. Omit **UN** before a scale word when the multiplier is exactly one. Compose larger numbers from highest scale to lowest: **MIL BI HUN TRI DEK KAR** = 1,234. The number phrase comes before the noun: **BI DEK TRI DAN** — 23 people.

Use **ORD** before a number to make an ordinal: **ORD UN** — first; **ORD BI** — second; **ORD TRI** — third. Use **DOT** between the whole and fractional parts of a decimal: **BI DOT PEN** = 2.5. For technical writing, Arabic numerals may be used where exact figures matter; read them aloud using the Edraen number words.

## 5. Adjectives, adverbs, and comparison

### Position

Descriptive roots normally come before the noun they modify:

- **LI SETH DAN** — the good person
- **NA THRAN DOM** — a large home
- **LI DURK VOR** — the dry water (grammatically formed but semantically contradictory unless a special context explains it)

The same descriptive root can follow **ES** to describe the subject:

- **LI DOM ES THRAN.** — The home is large.
- **LI VOR ES DURK.** — The water is dry.

Where the root's approved meaning permits it, a descriptive word may also modify a verb. Place it after the verb: **AN TUR RAL.** — I walk quickly. **RAL** retains its core meaning “fast”; the sentence position makes it adverbial.

### Comparison

- **SI** means “more” and introduces a comparative.
- **SU** means “most” and introduces a superlative.
- **AS** marks equality; **THAN** introduces the comparison target.

Examples:

- **AN ES SI THRAN THAN IR.** — I am larger than him/her.
- **IR ES SU THRAN.** — He/she/it is the largest.
- **AN ES AS SETH THAN ET.** — I am as good as you.

Do not add English-style adjective endings.

## 6. Basic sentence order

The default order is:

**SUBJECT + VERB + OBJECT + OPTIONAL DETAILS**

Examples:

- **AN DUV VEM.** — I eat food.
- **ET ZEK LI VOG.** — You see the bird.
- **IR SAVN AN.** — He/she/it remembers me.
- **EN VA BRAV BULK.** — We will carry a barrel.

Prepositional phrases and time/place details normally follow the core sentence: **AN GELR IN LI DARN.** — I live in the village.

### The verb ES: “be”

**ES** is an invariant verb used for identity, description, and location. It does not change for person or tense.

- **AN ES SETH.** — I am well/good.
- **IR ES LI TULK.** — He/she/it is the leader.
- **EN DA ES IN LI DOM.** — We were in the home.
- **UR VA ES TALM.** — They will be ready.

An existential statement (“there is/are”) begins with **ES** followed by the thing that exists: **ES NA DAN IN LI DOM.** — There is a person in the home.

## 7. Tense, aspect, negation, and modality

### Tense

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| No tense marker | Present, general, or contextually current | **AN BAK.** — I run. |
| **DA** | Past | **AN DA BAK.** — I ran. |
| **VA** | Future | **AN VA BAK.** — I will run. |

The verb itself never changes. Context words such as **DAK** (day), **RUK** (time), **VULR** (before), and **NETHL** (after) can specify when something happened.

### Aspect

Aspect describes the internal timing of an action:

| Marker | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **LO** | Ongoing or in progress | **AN LO FARL.** — I am speaking. |
| **PE** | Completed; with present relevance when no tense is marked | **AN PE DUV LI VEM.** — I have eaten the food. |
| **SA** | Habitual or usual | **AN SA TUR TO LI DARN.** — I usually walk to the village. |

Tense comes before aspect: **AN DA LO FARL.** — I was speaking. **AN VA LO FARL.** — I will be speaking. **AN DA PE DUV LI VEM.** — I had eaten the food.

**STIL** means that an action or state continues despite a change or reference point. Place it immediately before the main verb, after tense, aspect, and any modal: **AN VA LO STIL DALK.** — I will still be standing.

### Negation

**NU** precedes the tense, aspect, modal, and main verb sequence:

- **AN NU FIRV.** — I do not know.
- **AN NU DA BAK.** — I did not run.
- **AN NU VA GOVR.** — I will not go.
- **AN NU LO FARL.** — I am not speaking.

### Modality

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **TALR** | can; be able to | **AN TALR TUR.** — I can walk. |
| **ME** | must; have to | **AN ME TUR.** — I must walk. |
| **MO** | may; might | **AN MO TERV.** — I may come. |
| **SO** | should; ought to | **ET SO GARN EN.** — You should help us. |
| **WO** | would; hypothetical | **AN WO GARN ET.** — I would help you. |

The practical verb-phrase order is:

**NU + TENSE + ASPECT + MODAL + RI (if passive) + VERB**

**LET** introduces a permissive clause and comes before its subject: **LET MA KAVT KAVN UR MA TULV.** — Let kings keep their crowns. Unlike a normal command, the person or group being allowed to act is stated after **LET**.

Not every combination is equally natural. Use only the markers needed to express the intended meaning.

## 8. Questions and answers

### Yes/no questions

Place **HA** at the beginning and otherwise keep the statement order.

- **HA ET DUV VEM?** — Do you eat food?
- **HA ET DA ZEK LI VOG?** — Did you see the bird?
- **HA IR VA TERV?** — Will he/she/it come?
- **HA ET ES SETH?** — Are you well?

Short answers are **YE** (yes) and **NO** (no). For clarity, a full answer may repeat the verb phrase.

### Information questions

The question word goes at the beginning:

| Edraen | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **KA** | what |
| **KI** | who |
| **KO** | where |
| **KE** | when |
| **KU** | why |
| **KAI** | how |

The word **KE** also introduces a temporal clause meaning “when”: **KE LI THAK TERV** — when war comes. In a direct question it asks “when?” and begins the question as usual.

Examples:

- **KA ET DERV?** — What do you want?
- **KI ES LI TULK?** — Who is the leader?
- **KO ET GELR?** — Where do you live?
- **KE ET VA TERV?** — When will you come?
- **KU ET NU SAVN?** — Why do you not remember?
- **KAI AN KERV LI BRUK?** — How do I repair the door?

## 9. Commands, requests, and politeness

A command normally uses the bare verb, with the object after it. The subject “you” is understood.

- **TUR!** — Walk!
- **NU TUR!** — Do not walk!
- **PLE GARN AN.** — Please help me.
- **PLE LEVR LI ZULR TO EN.** — Please bring the book to us.

**PLE** comes before the command. A direct command is normal for urgent or familiar speech; **PLE** softens a request.

## 10. Prepositions and relations

Prepositions precede the noun phrase they relate to. Some lexical roots with a direct spatial or temporal meaning can also stand before a noun phrase, including **NARV** (“near”), **VULR** (“before”), and **NETHL** (“after”). These remain lexical roots, not grammar words.

| Edraen | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **AT** | at |
| **IN** | in; inside |
| **ON** | on |
| **TO** | to; toward |
| **OF** | of; related to; belonging to |
| **BY** | by; through the action of |
| **MI** | with |
| **FO** | for |
| **FU** | from |
| **UND** | under |
| **OV** | over; above |
| **THU** | through |
| **AR** | around |
| **BEH** | behind |
| **BET** | between |
| **SANS** | without |
| **TIL** | until |
| **SIN** | since |

Examples:

- **AN GELR IN LI DARN.** — I live in the village.
- **AN FARL MI ET.** — I speak with you.
- **IR TERV FU LI DARN.** — He/she/it comes from the village.
- **EN VA GOVR TO LI MOLN.** — We will go to the city.
- **LI ZULR ES ON LI NOLR.** — The book is on the table.

**TO** may precede a verb to introduce an infinitive or purpose complement, as in **AN DOVT TO TOVR** — I need to steal. The roots **VULR** (“before”) and **NETHL** (“after”) can also describe time relations: **AN TERV VULR DAK** — I come before day; **AN TERV NETHL DAK** — I come after day.

## 11. Joining phrases and clauses

### Coordination

- **AND** joins words, phrases, or clauses.
- **OR** gives alternatives.
- **BUT** marks contrast.
- **ALTH** introduces a concessive clause meaning “although/even though.”

Examples:

- **AN AND ET** — I and you.
- **HA ET DERV VEM OR VOR?** — Do you want food or water?
- **AN DERV LI ZULR, BUT AN NU HURV MELK.** — I want the book, but I do not have money.

### Reasons, conditions, and sequence

- **BEK** means “because.”
- **IF** introduces a condition.
- **THEN** marks the next step or result.
- **LET** introduces a permissive clause (“let/allow”).
- Use **THEN** for sequence or result and **BEK** for a reason.

Examples:

- **AN SAVM IN LI DOM BEK LI VOR ES KETHR.** — I stay in the home because the water is hot.
- **IF ET TERV, AN VA RAVN ET.** — If you come, I will meet you.
- **ET VA DUV LI VEM, THEN EN VA GOVR.** — You will eat the food, then we will go.
- **ALTH LI VOR ES KETHR, AN DUV LI VEM.** — Although the water is hot, I eat the food.
- **AN SAVM TIL DAK.** — I stay until day.
- **AN GELR IN LI DARN SIN VULR DAK.** — I have lived in the village since yesterday; use a more specific time expression when needed.

### Content clauses

**THA** introduces a clause that functions as the content of knowing, saying, thinking, or a similar verb.

- **AN FIRV THA IR ES SETH.** — I know that he/she/it is well.
- **IR LAVN THA ET VA TERV.** — He/she/it says that you will come.

A verb meaning “ask” may take an embedded question directly, without **THA**: **LI RATH THAVN KI MURV.** — The world asks who remained.

### Relative clauses

**TA** follows the noun it describes and introduces a clause that identifies it.

- **LI DAN TA AN SAVN** — the person whom I remember.
- **LI ZARV TA GARN EN** — the friend who helps us.
- **LI DOM TA IR DURN** — the home that he/she/it builds.

The noun immediately before **TA** is understood as the missing participant in the relative clause. Keep the clause simple when the intended role could be unclear.

## 12. Passive voice

Place **RI** before the verb. The affected thing becomes the grammatical subject. **BY** may introduce the agent.

- **LI DAN RI SAVN.** — The person is remembered.
- **LI DAN RI SAVN BY AN.** — The person is remembered by me.
- **LI DOM DA RI DURN BY EN.** — The home was built by us.

Active voice is preferred when it is clear and natural. Use the passive when the affected person or thing matters more than the actor, or when the actor is unknown.

## 13. Word formation and compounds

The two approved compound patterns remain in force:

1. **Describing root + main concept root.** The final root names the main concept. Example: **ED + RATH = EDRATH**, “The Primordial World.”
2. **Main concept root + state/quality root.** The first root identifies the concept; the second gives its defining state or quality. Example: **RATH + AEN = RATHAEN**, “The Eternal World.”

Roots join directly by default. Do not insert linking vowels or endings unless a documented rule requires them. Compound meanings should be interpretable from the component meanings and the approved pattern. A compound may acquire a conventional or figurative meaning, but that meaning must be recorded rather than assumed.

### Derivation and category

- Do not add English endings such as -s, -ed, or -ing to Edraen roots.
- Plural, tense, aspect, and modality use the separate forms described in this guide.
- A root may be used as a noun, verb, adjective, or adverb only when its approved meaning supports that use. Position may permit ordinary descriptive use, but it must not reverse or arbitrarily redefine the root's meaning.
- New derivational affixes should be added only after they have been documented and checked for collisions with roots and grammar words.

## 14. Time and everyday reference

The current vocabulary supports simple time expressions:

- **DAK** — day
- **NHUR** — night
- **GUR** — dawn
- **BEMRA** — morning
- **MAVATH** — afternoon
- **MORALA** — evening
- **DETH** — dusk
- **RUK** — time
- **HURK** — hour
- **MIRK** — minute
- **SIRN** — second
- **RUVN** — week
- **MELT** — month
- **YELN** — year
- **MARN** — age or span of time

Combine them with numbers and prepositions: **BI HURK** — two hours; **IN TRI DAK** — in three days. **DI DAK** (“this day”) means “today” when context is clear; **VULR DAK** (“before day”) means “yesterday,” and **NETHL DAK** (“after day”) means “tomorrow,” when the reference point is today. **IN BEMRA**, **IN MAVATH**, and **IN MORALA** mean “in the morning,” “in the afternoon,” and “in the evening.”

## 16. Standardization and collision policy

The standard is designed to be regular, learnable, and extensible. It is not necessary to claim that a language can never change; the requirement is that every current form has a clear, documented role.

Before adding a root or grammar word:

1. Check its exact spelling against the complete lexical inventory.
2. Check it against every grammar word and established whole term.
3. Confirm that the form is not already assigned another meaning.
4. Record its meaning, word class, and pronunciation guide.
5. Update the phase record, master dictionary, grammar ledger, and canon registry together.

Similar sounds are permitted; exact duplicate spellings are not. Roots and grammar words are separate inventories, but **no exact spelling collision between them is allowed**.

This document defines the practical standard grammar for everyday Edraen. Standard v3.0 clarifies noun-phrase order, number formation, embedded questions, temporal relations, and new everyday vocabulary. It also adds **SOM**, **ANY**, **SANS**, **TIL**, **SIN**, **ALTH**, and **DOT**. Future vocabulary additions remain welcome, but grammar must not be improvised during translation; update this standard first when a rule needs to change.

## 17. Noun-phrase order and quantity

Use this general order as a reliable default:

**DETERMINER OR POSSESSOR + NUMBER/PLURAL + OTHER/DEGREE + DESCRIPTION + NOUN**

The determiner position is normally occupied by only one of these: **LI**, **NA**, a demonstrative (**DI/DU/DE/DO**), a possessive pronoun, or a universal/indefinite determiner (**AL**, **OTH**, **SOM**, **ANY**). Do not stack competing determiners.

- **LI MA SETH DAN** — the good people.
- **DE MA SETH DAN** — these good people.
- **AN MA THARN** — my enemies.
- **SOM MA DAN** — some people.
- **ANY VEM** — any food.
- **AL MA OTH DAN** — all the other people.
- **BI SETH DOM** — two good homes.
- **LI SU THRAN DOM** — the largest home.

Use **MA** for an unspecified plural count noun. Do not use **MA** after an explicit number or a plural demonstrative: **BI DAN** (“two people”), **DE DAN** (“these people”). Mass nouns such as water and food normally appear without plural marking unless the intended meaning is a countable kind or serving.

**SOM** means “some” and **ANY** means “any.” **ANY** is especially natural in questions, negative clauses, and conditions: **HA ET HURV ANY MELK?** (“Do you have any money?”); **AN NU HURV ANY MELK.** (“I do not have any money.”) For quantity questions, place **KAI** (“how”) immediately before the noun phrase: **KAI MA DAN?** — “How many people?”; **KAI MELK?** — “How much money?” In a full sentence, the quantity phrase remains at the beginning: **KAI MA DAN ES IN LI DARN?** — “How many people are in the village?”

## 18. Verb complements and infinitives

Use **TO + bare verb** after a verb of desire, need, planning, attempt, or a similar intention when the second action is expressed:

- **AN DERV TO TUR.** — I want to walk.
- **AN DOVT TO SAVR.** — I need to learn.
- **AN SAVK TO GOVR.** — I plan to go.
- **AN FALD TO KERV LI BRUK.** — I try to repair the door.

The ability root **TALR** and the grammar modals **ME**, **MO**, **SO**, and **WO** take a bare verb without **TO**:

- **AN TALR FARL EDRAEN.** — I can speak Edraen.
- **AN ME GOVR.** — I must go.
- **ET SO SAVN.** — You should remember.
- **AN MO TERV.** — I may come.
- **AN WO GARN ET.** — I would help you.

Use **TO** for purpose as well: **AN GOVR TO LI RAGAM FO RAG.** — I go to the office for work. If a sentence contains two consecutive **TO** phrases, use a comma or rewrite it if the relationship is unclear.

## 19. Embedded questions and reference clauses

A direct question uses the question word at the beginning. An embedded question keeps its question word at the beginning of its own clause but does not use **HA**:

- **KO ET GELR?** — Where do you live?
- **AN FIRV KO ET GELR.** — I know where you live.
- **AN NU FIRV KU IR THULR.** — I do not know why he/she/it left.
- **ET THAVN KE EN VA TERV.** — You ask when we will come.

**THA** introduces a statement used as the content of another clause: **AN FIRV THA IR ES SETH.** — I know that he/she/it is well. **TA** introduces a relative clause after its noun: **LI DAN TA AN SAVN** — the person whom I remember. The noun before **TA** is understood as the missing participant in the relative clause. If a long relative clause could be read in two ways, repeat the noun or rewrite the sentence.

## 20. Temporal reference, dates, and clock time

Use existing time roots and numbered periods rather than inventing unapproved weekday or month names:

- **DI DAK** — today.
- **VULR DAK** — yesterday / the day before the current reference day.
- **NETHL DAK** — tomorrow / the day after the current reference day.
- **IN BEMRA** — in the morning.
- **IN MAVATH** — in the afternoon.
- **IN MORALA** — in the evening.
- **AT BI HURK** — at two o'clock / at the second hour.
- **AT BI HURK AND TRI DEK MIRK** — at 2:30 (two hours and thirty minutes).
- **ORD UN MELT** — the first month.
- **UN DAK IN MELT** — one day in the month; use an ordinal before the month when the day number must be exact.

Edraen has no fixed canon calendar at this stage. Numbered days and months are a neutral way to give dates until a setting-specific calendar is established. Use **DOT** in decimals: **UN DOT PEN** = 1.5. Arabic numerals are permitted in technical, financial, and time-sensitive writing when they improve precision.

## 22. Spoken and written style

The standard grammar is used for ordinary conversation and clear prose. The same core rules apply to casual, formal, and poetic writing; register changes vocabulary choice, sentence length, and how directly a speaker phrases a request.

- Use **PLE** to soften requests. A bare imperative is acceptable for urgent, familiar, or direct commands.
- Repeat a noun instead of using a pronoun when the referent might be unclear.
- Short clauses are preferred in conversation. Longer subordinate clauses are acceptable in writing when their boundaries are clear.
- Put direct speech inside quotation marks and use the ordinary punctuation rules. Capitalize proper names, but do not assign them an Edraen etymology unless one is recorded.
- A new borrowed word or newly coined compound is not automatically standard. Record its spelling, meaning, pronunciation, and role before treating it as approved vocabulary.

## 23. Scope of the standard

Edraen Standard v3.0 is a stable, usable core for everyday speech, practical writing, connected narrative, and formal passages. It defines the regular grammar rather than trying to freeze all future vocabulary or culture. The language can grow without being considered unfinished every time a new technical term is needed.

The following are deliberately left open to future specification rather than silently guessed: regional dialects, historical sound changes, a canonical calendar, a detailed politeness hierarchy, and specialist terminology for fields not yet represented in the dictionary. These are expansion areas, not blockers for ordinary use.
