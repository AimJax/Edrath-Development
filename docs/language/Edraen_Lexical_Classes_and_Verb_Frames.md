# EDRAEN LEXICAL CLASSES AND VERB FRAMES

**Document:** Working Lexical-Syntax Supplement  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Descriptive supplement; no new root meanings or grammar rules are approved by this document  
**Standard under reference:** Edraen Standard v3.1

Edraen has 628 approved lexical roots, but a large root inventory does not by itself tell a learner which sentence patterns each verb accepts. This supplement records the frames already supported by the grammar and examples. It distinguishes attested patterns from careful extensions that still need testing.

## 1. Core principles

1. Roots do not inflect for person, tense, number, or grammatical role.
2. A root's approved meaning limits how it can be used. A noun meaning “stone” does not automatically become a verb meaning “to stone” unless that verb sense is separately approved.
3. A root may have more than one lexical class only when its approved meaning supports those uses. Do not infer unrestricted noun–verb–adjective conversion.
4. A verb's meaning does not automatically establish every possible object or complement. Use the frames below where documented; mark other frames as untested.
5. Future root proposals should record a primary lexical class. Verb proposals should also record whether the verb is intransitive, takes a direct object, or selects a clause or phrase complement. This metadata should be added deliberately rather than guessed for all existing roots.

## 2. Frame notation

- **S** — subject
- **V** — verb
- **NP** — noun phrase
- **VP** — verb phrase
- **THA clause** — a full clause introduced by THA
- **Embedded question** — a question clause introduced by its own question word
- **TO + recipient NP** — a prepositional phrase marking the recipient or destination

The frames below describe syntax, not English word-for-word order.

## 3. Supported construction types

| Frame | Function | Example | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| S + V | Intransitive or complete predicate | **IR TERV.** — He/she/it comes. | Standard Grammar |
| S + V + NP | Direct object | **AN DUV LI VEM.** — I eat the food. | Standard Grammar |
| S + V + TO + VP | Infinitive or purpose complement | **AN DERV TO TUR.** — I want to walk. | Standard Grammar |
| S + V + LI + VP | Activity noun phrase as complement | **AN LIRV LI DUV BAVR.** — I like eating meat. | Standard Grammar v3.1 |
| S + V + THA + clause | Content clause | **AN FIRV THA IR ES SETH.** — I know that he/she/it is well. | Standard Grammar |
| S + V + embedded question | Indirect question | **AN FIRV KO ET GELR.** — I know where you live. | Standard Grammar |
| S + V + NP + TO + NP | Object plus recipient/destination | **AN VA DARV NA SAVAT TO ET.** — I will send an email to you. | Phrasebook/Standard Grammar |
| NP + RI + V + BY + agent | Passive clause | **LI DAN RI SAVN BY AN.** — The person is remembered by me. | Standard Grammar |
| S + ES + description/location | Copular predicate | **AN ES SETH.** — I am well. | Standard Grammar |
| ES + NP + location | Existential statement | **ES NA DAN IN LI DOM.** — There is a person in the home. | Standard Grammar |

**TO has more than one function.** Before a noun phrase it marks a destination or recipient (“to you”); before a bare verb phrase it marks an infinitive or purpose complement. The following word determines which pattern is present.

## 4. High-frequency verb-frame register

This register is deliberately limited to verbs whose patterns are shown in the current grammar, phrasebook, or translation benchmark. It is not a complete valency dictionary.

| Root | Approved meaning | Documented frame or frames | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DERV** | want | NP; TO + VP; THA + full clause | The THA clause allows a different subject. |
| **DOVT** | need | NP; TO + VP | Do not assume it takes every type of clause complement. |
| **LIRV** | like | NP; TO + VP; LI + VP activity phrase | LI + VP gives an activity reading; TO + VP gives an infinitive reading. |
| **SAVK** | plan | NP; TO + VP | Only these patterns are established here. |
| **FALD** | try; make an attempt | TO + VP | No additional complement frame is implied. |
| **TALR** | can; be able | Bare VP | Do not add TO after TALR under the current standard. |
| **FIRV** | know | NP; THA clause; embedded question | These cover a known fact, a proposition, and an indirect question. |
| **LAVN** | say | THA clause | Example: **IR LAVN THA ET VA TERV.** |
| **THAVN** | ask | Embedded question | Example: **ET THAVN KE EN VA TERV.** |
| **DARV** | send | NP + TO recipient/destination | Example: **AN VA DARV NA SAVAT TO ET.** |
| **LEVR** | bring | NP + TO recipient/destination | Example: **PLE LEVR LI ZULR TO EN.** |
| **DOVR** | give; transfer something to another | NP + TO recipient is tested in the benchmark | This is a compositional frame to validate, not an exhaustive valency claim. |
| **DUV** | eat | NP | Example: **AN DUV LI VEM.** |
| **GARN** | help | NP | Example: **PLE GARN AN.** |
| **SAVN** | remember | NP; object gap in a relative clause | Example: **LI DAN TA AN SAVN.** |

The register does not mean that every verb with a similar English translation takes the same frames. New verbs should be checked against their own approved meanings and documented examples.

## 5. Activity phrases versus infinitives

The distinction established in Standard Grammar v3.1 is important:

- **AN LIRV TO DUV BAVR.** — I like to eat meat.
- **AN LIRV LI DUV BAVR.** — I like eating meat.

The first treats eating as an action complement of “like.” The second treats the activity itself as a noun-like phrase. For the negative variants, keep the intended scope explicit:

- **AN NU LIRV LI DUV BAVR.** — I do not like eating meat.
- **AN LIRV LI NU DUV BAVR.** — I like not eating meat.

Do not create an English-style -ing suffix or infer that every verb accepts LI + VP.

## 6. What remains open

The current frame register does not yet define every verb's argument structure. In particular:

- whether **NULR** (“stop”) accepts LI + VP, TO + VP, or another complement;
- how counterfactual past meanings combine with tense, aspect, and WO;
- how several embedded clauses should be punctuated and parsed when nested;
- how multiple modal meanings stack, including a modal with **TALR**;
- whether additional roots permit both a direct object and a recipient phrase.

These are language-design questions, not vocabulary gaps. Add a frame only when the meaning and syntax have been tested, and record the example in the [Translation Benchmark](Edraen_Translation_Benchmark.md).
