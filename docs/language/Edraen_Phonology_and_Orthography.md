# EDRAEN PHONOLOGY AND ORTHOGRAPHY

**Document:** Working Sound-System Specification  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Provisional technical standard  
**Scope:** Pronunciation, syllable structure, stress, and spelling; no root spelling or lexical meaning is changed here.

This document turns the existing pronunciation notes and the 629-root inventory into a consistent working system. The IPA values below are explicit recommendations so that Edraen can be pronounced consistently. They remain provisional until the language creator confirms or revises them. The dictionary's recorded pronunciation guide continues to take priority for an established word.

## 1. Vowel system

| Spelling | Proposed IPA | Practical guide |
|---|---|---|
| A | /ɑ/ | approximately the vowel in *father* |
| E | /ɛ/ | approximately the vowel in *bed* |
| I | /i/ | approximately the vowel in *machine* |
| O | /ɔ/ | approximately the vowel in *thought* for speakers who distinguish it from *father* |
| U | /u/ | approximately the vowel in *rule* |
| AE | /eɪ/ | approximately the vowel in *day* |
| AU | /aʊ/ | approximately the vowel in *now* |

AE and AU are diphthongs, but each counts as **one syllable nucleus**. Do not split AEN or AETH into extra syllables because of AE.

Vowel length is not contrastive in the current standard. Do not use length alone to distinguish words. The proposed values are targets rather than demands for an exact English accent; regional pronunciation may vary without changing a word's identity.

## 2. Consonant system

The following table lists the consonant values recommended for native roots in the current inventory.

| Spelling | Proposed IPA | Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| B | /b/ | as in *bat* |
| D | /d/ | as in *day* |
| F | /f/ | as in *fine* |
| G | /g/ | always hard, as in *go* |
| H | /h/ | as in *home* |
| K | /k/ | as in *skin* |
| KH | /x/ | voiceless velar fricative, like Scottish *loch* |
| L | /l/ | as in *lip* |
| M | /m/ | as in *man* |
| N | /n/ | as in *net* |
| P | /p/ | as in *spin* |
| R | /ɾ/ | proposed single tongue-tip tap, similar to the middle consonant in many pronunciations of *butter* |
| S | /s/ | as in *sun* |
| T | /t/ | as in *stop* |
| TH | /θ/ | as in *thin*, never the sound in *this* |
| V | /v/ | as in *vine* |
| Y | /j/ | as in *yes* |
| Z | /z/ | as in *zoo* |

The proposed R value is a tap /ɾ/, not an English approximant /ɹ/. This is a deliberate working choice, not a claim that the older materials already settled the issue.

The spelling NH has a special, limited reading: in the established root **NHUR**, it is pronounced /n/. It is not a separate consonant phoneme in the current analysis. Do not generalize this spelling to new roots without review.

The current native-root inventory does not use C, J, Q, W, or X as letters. KH represents /x/ even though the IPA symbol looks like an X; it does not authorize the letter X in Edraen spelling.

## 3. Syllables and root shapes

Counting AE and AU as single nuclei, the 629-root inventory contains:

- **603 monosyllabic roots**
- **25 disyllabic roots**
- **1 trisyllabic root: MORALA**

This count describes the current inventory; it is not a requirement that every future word be monosyllabic.

A useful general syllable template is **(C)(C)V(C)(C)**, where V can be A, E, I, O, U, AE, or AU. The template is a guide, not a license to invent any possible cluster. Native roots should follow the attested patterns below unless a deliberate exception is approved.

### Attested initial consonant clusters

The current roots attest these word-initial clusters:

**BR, DR, FR, GR, KR, PR, SK, THR**

In THR, TH represents one consonant /θ/, so the cluster is /θr/. Do not add new initial clusters casually.

### Attested word-final consonant clusters

The following written sequences occur at the ends of approved roots. TH is one consonant unit, so forms such as LTH represent two consonants, /lθ/, not three.

**DL, DV, KM, KR, KV, LD, LK, LM, LN, LR, LS, LT, LTH, LV, ML, MR, MTH, MV, NL, NM, NR, NV, RD, RG, RK, RL, RM, RN, RT, RTH, RV, SH, SK, THK, THL, THM, THN, THR, THS, THV, VD, VK, VL, VM, VN, VR, VS, VT, VTH**

These are **attested root endings**, not a guarantee that every one is equally easy to pronounce or productive in new words. A proposed root with a new coda cluster requires review rather than automatic acceptance.

Vowel-initial roots such as ED, AEN, and AETH are valid. Do not insert a leading consonant to force them into a consonant-initial template.

## 4. Stress

The default stress falls on the **penultimate syllable** of a word:

- A one-syllable root is stressed on its only syllable.
- A two-syllable root is stressed on its first syllable.
- A three-syllable root such as MORALA is stressed on its second syllable.

For a compound, apply the default to the complete compound as one written word unless an established pronunciation guide specifies otherwise. Do not add a linking vowel to make a compound easier to say. If a new compound creates an uncertain or difficult sequence, record the pronunciation question and review it instead of silently changing the spelling.

Proper names and dictionary entries with a recorded special pronunciation guide retain that guide.

## 5. Orthographic rules

1. Write lexical roots and established compounds without changing their approved spelling.
2. Write grammar words separately.
3. Treat AE, AU, TH, and KH as fixed spelling units when reading and syllabifying words.
4. Treat NH as /n/ only in NHUR under the current convention.
5. Use the dictionary pronunciation guide when it gives a specific reading.
6. Do not add letters, remove consonants, or insert linking vowels to repair a difficult sequence.
7. Capitalization in dictionary headwords is for reference. Ordinary Edraen sentences use normal sentence capitalization.

## 6. Boundaries and compounds

Compounds join roots directly. Their boundary does not itself add a syllable or a new sound. Pronounce each component according to the working sound values while preserving the written form. The two approved compound patterns and their meanings remain defined in the Language Foundation and Standard Grammar.

The inventory-derived clusters above describe approved roots, not every sequence that can occur when roots are joined. A compound that creates a new boundary sequence is therefore **readable by the component rules but not automatically evidence that the same sequence is allowed inside a new root**.

## 7. Known limits

This working specification makes pronunciation more consistent, but it does not establish historical sound changes, dialects, poetic meter, phonological alternations, or a full theory of how compounds may simplify in rapid speech. None of those processes should be assumed during translation. Add them only when they are deliberately designed and documented.

**Revision rule:** if a proposed pronunciation conflicts with a dictionary entry or a previously approved root, do not silently alter the root. Record the conflict and resolve it explicitly.
