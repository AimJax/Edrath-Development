# EDRAEN DICTIONARY — LEARNER'S EDITION & MASTER INVENTORY

**Status:** Approved Canon  
**Purpose:** Canonical cumulative reference for every approved Edraen root  
**Inventory:** 485 approved lexical roots  
**Root batches:** 7 foundational roots + 393 additions in Phases 1–6 + 85 additions in Phase 7

## Canon boundary

This is the single cumulative, learner-facing dictionary for all 485 approved Edraen lexical roots from the foundational set and Phases 1–7. Use it as the everyday reference; the phase files are historical batch records. Entries retain approved meanings and reader-friendly pronunciation guides. The root list is alphabetized and grouped by initial letter for casual reading and lookup. Standalone grammar words are documented separately in the [Standard Grammar & Usage](Edraen_Standard_Grammar.md) guide and are not counted as lexical roots.

Root definitions are lexical only. They do not independently establish religions, metaphysics, histories, social institutions, cultural practices, or facts about the peoples of Edrath.

## Quick start: reading and using Edraen

Edraen Standard v2 supports ordinary statements, questions, commands, basic conversation, and connected clauses. The full rule set and phrase examples are in the [Edraen Standard Grammar & Usage](Edraen_Standard_Grammar.md) and [Everyday Phrasebook](Edraen_Everyday_Phrasebook.md).

### Basic sentence order

**Subject + (NU) + (DA or VA) + (aspect/modal) + verb + object**

- Default order is **subject → verb → object**.
- No tense marker means present or general action.
- **DA** marks past; **VA** marks future.
- **NU** negates the verb phrase.
- **LO** marks an action in progress; **PE** marks a completed action; **SA** marks habitual action.
- Verbs keep their dictionary form; they do not conjugate for person or tense.
- Articles and modifiers precede nouns: **LI SETH DAN** — “the good person.”
- **MA** marks an unspecified plural: **LI MA DAN** — “the people.”
- A possessor precedes the noun: **AN ZULR** — “my book.”

## Approved pronunciation conventions

- Core vowels: A, E, I, O, U.
- AE is approximately the vowel in English “day”; AU is approximately the vowel in English “now.”
- TH is pronounced as in English “thin.”
- KH is pronounced like the final consonant in Scottish “loch.”
- NH in NHUR is pronounced as N.
- Default stress falls on the penultimate syllable, except that established proper names retain their recorded pronunciation guides.
- The guides in the table are practical reader aids, not IPA or a complete phonological specification.
- Permitted syllable shapes and the full consonant-cluster rules have not yet been established.

## Lexical distinctions

- **DUR** = soil or earth as material; **KEL** = land or territory; **RATH** = world or encompassing realm.
- **KORV** = wood as material; **TOL** = tree.
- **KELT** = plant root; **ZAL** = seed; **MEL** = flower; **ZAV** = grass.
- **MAV** = animal as a broad category; **ZIVR** = fish; **GUV** = insect; **KESH** = reptile; **VOG** = bird.
- **VETH** = ice; **KHAL** = snow; **ZUR** = rain; **SUL** = fog or mist.
- **DAK** = day; **RUK** = time; **MARN** = age/span of time; **KAD** = beginning; **ED** = primordial/original.
- **KREN** = mind; **TAV** = thought; **GREM** = memory; **VARN** = will; **THRUK** = knowledge; **ZOVR** = wisdom.
- **DASK** = law; **KURV** = authority; **THREN** = group of people; **DAN** = individual person.
- **MALK** = metal generally; **THORG** = iron specifically.
- **KREG** = craft; **ZEG** = skill; **RAG** = work.
- **LETH** = love/deep affection; **YAR** = joy/happiness; **GRATH** = fear; **VURN** = anger; **METHR** = sorrow/grief.
- **SURN** is a word for spirit/soul; its existence or nature is not established by the vocabulary entry.
- **DAN** refers to an individual person. Whether it applies to any particular kind of being depends on the wider setting.
- **MERV** means make or create generally; **DURN** means build or assemble specifically.
- **BRAV** means carry; **LEVR** means bring toward a place or person; **DOVR** means give or transfer to another.
- **THAR** means sea; **THARK** means ship. Their spellings are distinct and their pronunciations should remain clear.
- **SULR** means quiet; **SILR** means read. The similar forms are distinct roots.
- **KEL** means land or territory; **KELR** means change, **KELV** means deep, and **KELN** means agree. These meanings are distinct.

- **GEL** = head; **GELN** = rough; **GELM** = clay; **GELV** = ladder. The spellings are distinct and their meanings should remain clear in use.
- **DULV** = bitter; **DULTH** = sour; **DULN** = cheese. These similar forms are separate approved roots.
- **ZELN** = guard; **ZELR** = map; **ZELTH** = early. These similar forms are separate approved roots.
- **SILN** = loud; **SILR** = read. Their similar forms should be kept distinct in pronunciation.
- **MELR** = lush; **MEL** = flower; **MELTH** = ash; **MELN** = ink; **MELV** = artisan.
- **VURK** = heal or recover; **ZURV** = cure or bring about recovery. Their meanings are related but not identical.
