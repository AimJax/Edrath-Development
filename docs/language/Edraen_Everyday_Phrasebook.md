## Introductions and identity

| Edraen | English |
|---|---|
| **AN ES [name].** | I am [name]. |
| **KI ES ET?** | Who are you? |
| **AN ES NA SAVTH.** | I am a student. |
| **AN FARL EDRAEN.** | I speak Edraen. |
| **AN TALR FARL EDRAEN.** | I can speak Edraen. |

A name may be inserted as a proper name. Do not invent an Edraen etymology for a name unless one has been approved.

## Complex sentence practice

These examples use the additional constructions documented in Edraen Standard v3.2. They are compositional grammar examples, not fixed idioms.

| Edraen | English |
|---|---|
| **IF AN DA PE FIRV, AN DA PE WO TERV.** | If I had known, I would have come. |
| **LI DAN TA LAVN THA ET WO TERV DA THULR.** | The person who said that you would come left. |
| **AN DA NULR LI DUV BAVR.** | I stopped eating meat. |
| **IR DA LAVN: “AN VA THERV NETHL DAK.”** | He/she/it said, “I will return tomorrow.” |
| **AN DA PE MO NU TALR GOVR.** | I might not have been able to go. |

Remember the scope distinctions:

- **DA PE WO** marks the hypothetical completed past result in a counterfactual condition.
- **TA** introduces a relative clause; **THA** introduces the content of a verb such as **LAVN**.
- In direct quotation, first-person pronouns and relative time words belong to the original utterance's perspective.
- **MO NU TALR GOVR** means “might not be able to go”: the negation belongs inside the modal's scope.

## Practical notes

- **AN**, **ET**, **EN**, **IR**, and **UR** work as subjects and objects; their spelling does not change.
- **NU** means “not” inside a clause. **NO** is the standalone answer “no.”
- **DA** marks past time; **VA** marks future time; **LO**, **PE**, and **SA** mark aspect.
- Use **LI** for “the,” **NA** for “a/an,” and **MA** for an unspecified plural.
- **SOM** means “some,” **ANY** means “any,” **SANS** means “without,” **TIL** means “until,” **SIN** means “since,” and **ALTH** means “although.”
- Use **DOT** between number words when reading a decimal, such as **BI DOT PEN** (“2.5”).
- **PLE** comes before a request or command.
- **LI + verb phrase** can name an activity: **LI DUV BAVR** means “eating meat” as an activity; **AN LIRV LI DUV BAVR** means “I like eating meat.”
- The examples are standard grammatical phrases, not a claim that every one has become a fixed cultural idiom.
